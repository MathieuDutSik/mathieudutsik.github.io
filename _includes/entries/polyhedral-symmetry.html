A polytope can be given in two ways: by its vertices, or by the inequalities of its facets. Going from one description to the other is the dual description problem, a basic task of computational geometry, solved in general by programs such as lrs and cdd. The size of the output can grow exponentially, and for the polytopes that appear in the geometry of numbers or in combinatorial optimization these programs often run out of memory or time.

These polytopes, however, have very large symmetry groups, and only one facet per orbit is needed. I developed, step by step, a set of methods that work up to symmetry. The adjacency decomposition method, which comes from Christof and Reinelt, explores the graph of the orbits of facets: starting from one facet, it computes the facets adjacent to it, keeps those in new orbits, and continues. Each step is a dual description problem in one dimension less, with the stabilizer of the facet as symmetry group, so the method can be applied recursively. Making this work at scale required many components: fast tests of equivalence of facets under the group, choosing at each step between a direct computation and a further decomposition, computing the symmetry group of a polyhedron with graph automorphism software, and running the computation in parallel. The methods are now in the C++ library polyhedral_common.

Two computations show what this makes possible:

  • Perfect forms in dimension 8. Perfect forms are the vertices of Voronoi's theory of lattice packings (see the Hermite constant in dimension 9). With Achill Schürmann and Frank Vallentin, we classified them in dimension 8 and found 10,916 of them. The hardest step was the dual description of a cone in dimension 36 with 120 extreme rays, coming from the lattice E8: it has about 25,075,566,937,584 facets, which fall into 83,092 orbits. The classification was later used to compute the cohomology of GLN(Z) for N ≥ 8 and to prove that the algebraic K-group K8(Z) is trivial.
  • The contact polytope of the Leech lattice. The Leech lattice, in dimension 24, is the most remarkable lattice of the geometry of numbers. Its contact polytope is the convex hull of its 196,560 minimal vectors. It has 1,197,362,269,604,214,277,200 facets, in 232 orbits under the automorphism group.
