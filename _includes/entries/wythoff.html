The Wythoff construction takes an n-dimensional polytope P, not necessarily convex, and a non-empty subset S of {0, …, n}, and returns a new n-dimensional polytope W(P,S), called a Wythoffian. Its vertices correspond to the flags of faces of P whose dimensions are given by S. Many classical operations are special cases: S = {0} gives P itself, S = {n} its dual, S = {1} the medial polytope, and S = {0, …, n} the most truncated form. Applied to the regular polytopes, the construction produces many of the uniform polytopes, and it keeps the full symmetry group of P.

Embedding into hypercubes. A graph is L1-embeddable if its path metric, possibly multiplied by a constant, can be realized by points of a hypercube with the Hamming distance. This property has links with cuts, with optimization and with the design of interconnection networks, and only special graphs have it. With Michel Deza and Sergey Shpectorov, we studied the skeletons of the Wythoffians W(P,S) of the regular polytopes P. We obtained a list of the L1-embeddable ones that we conjecture to be complete, with explicit embeddings.

Homology of the Mathieu groups. The homology of a finite group G is computed from a free resolution of Z over the group ring ZG, which is hard to build for large groups. With Graham Ellis, we used the Wythoff construction to build such a resolution. If G acts on a Wythoff polytope, the polytope gives a contractible cell complex with a G-action, whose cells have known stabilizers. Perturbation techniques, implemented in Ellis' GAP package HAP, then turn it into an explicit free ZG-resolution. This gave, for example, H3(M24, Z) = Z12 and H5(M23, Z) = Z7 for the Mathieu sporadic simple groups. The method applies in principle to any finite group with a good geometric action.
